6. Code walkthrough5:30-7:15About 20 seconds per file
On screen: VS Code: UnderwritingClaimService, UnderwritingBatchSender, UnderwritingRetryJob, UnderwritingDecisionResource, DecisionProcessor.
UnderwritingClaimService.cls, at the two FOR UPDATE queries.

The claim locks the rows, reads them again under the lock, and only moves the ones still Ready. In my org I started two send runs at the same moment: one batch ran, the other claimed nothing, and all 1,000 applications had a single batch key.

UnderwritingBatchSender.cls, at sharedKey and retryBatch.

This sends one batch. The Idempotency-Key is created once per batch and stored on each application. On a 429, a 5xx or a timeout, the same batch is retried with the same key, however long the wait, which is what FundMate confirmed: the partner ignores a repeated key for 24 hours. When a user resends after fixing something, the key is cleared, so the partner never ignores a corrected application.

UnderwritingRetryJob.cls, at execute.

The retry job resends exactly those applications after the wait: a delayed job for up to 10 minutes, a one-off scheduled job for longer. Retry-After wins, otherwise exponential backoff with jitter. After five attempts the application is marked Send Failed and goes to Integration Exceptions.

UnderwritingDecisionResource.cls, at the SignatureVerifier.verify call.

The inbound endpoint only accepts the integration user: the partner gets a token with OAuth client credentials, and any other token, even mine as admin, gets a 403. Then it checks an HMAC-SHA256 signature over the timestamp and the raw body before parsing anything. A missing, wrong or old signature gets a 401.

DecisionProcessor.cls, at the process method's steps.

Every decision gets its own result. It checks the shape, skips duplicates, matches the application and its tracking id, checks offer ids, then saves with partial success, so one bad decision never blocks the others.

That covers sending and receiving in code. Let me show decisions arriving for real.

The case

FundMate funds small businesses based on their bank cash flow. A business applies, an outside underwriting service reviews it, and a Funding Specialist works the result. The task is to build the Salesforce side of that loop.

Salesforce (what I built)Holds businesses, owners and bank statements. Builds applications, validates them, sends them, receives decisions, creates offers, routes work.
Underwriting APIOutside partner. Takes up to 50 applications per call (API key). Sends signed decisions back later.
Funding SpecialistsWork decided applications from queues. Compare offers. Can't see others' applications or change Status by hand.

FundMate's public rules used in the build: average $10,000 a month over 3 months of statements; terms 3 to 18 months; daily, weekly or monthly payments. Sample businesses are in NY, TX, FL and CA, all with commercial financing disclosure laws, so each offer shows payback, payment and an estimated APR.

Questions I asked Deepak

Is "end of day Wednesday" November 4 or 11?

November 4.

How is each request signed: header, algorithm, coverage?

Default confirmed: hex HMAC-SHA256 in X-Signature over X-Timestamp + "." + body. Older than 5 minutes rejected; 401 when missing, wrong or too old.

Does the batch endpoint take an idempotency key, so a retry after a timeout can't duplicate?

Default confirmed: reuse the same Idempotency-Key on every retry of a batch; the partner ignores a repeat for 24 hours.

Which queues exist, and what decides where an application goes?

Default confirmed (one queue per decision, over $250,000 to Senior), plus a new Integration Exceptions queue for partner rejections and send failures.

Likely interview questions

Why Batch Apex and not chained Queueables?

Each execute gets fresh limits and one callout per 50. Chained Queueables hit Developer Edition's chain depth, and one transaction allows only 120 s of callouts.

How do you stop two runs sending the same application?

FOR UPDATE locks the Ready rows, the claim re-reads them under the lock and only moves the ones still Ready to Queued with its batch key, before any callout. The second run finds nothing Ready.

What if the callout times out but the partner got it?

The retry sends the same applications with the same Idempotency-Key, so the partner ignores the repeat for 24 hours.

Why does the log survive a rollback?

Logs are published as a platform event set to Publish Immediately; a subscriber writes Integration_Log__c in its own transaction.

Why verify the signature before parsing?

The HMAC is over the raw bytes. Parsing first wastes work on forged requests and re-serialising could change the bytes.

Why are some classes without sharing?

System work must see every relevant record (claiming queue-owned apps, writing results, logging). Each of the 11 says why in its header; user-facing code runs in user mode.

What would you change for production?

Shield Platform Encryption instead of Classic, Retry-After as an HTTP date, a real partner sandbox, monitoring dashboards on Integration_Log__c, and a managed package with a namespace.

You used AI tools. Can you explain every part?

Yes. Walk through the claim, the retry key, the signature check and DecisionProcessor without notes.
