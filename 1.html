6. Code walkthrough5:30-7:15About 20 seconds per file
On screen: VS Code: UnderwritingClaimService, UnderwritingBatchSender, UnderwritingRetryJob, UnderwritingDecisionResource, DecisionProcessor.
UnderwritingClaimService.cls, at the two FOR UPDATE queries.

The claim locks the rows, reads them again under the lock, and only moves the ones still Ready. In my org I started two send runs at the same moment: one batch ran, the other claimed nothing, and all 1,000 applications had a single batch key.

UnderwritingBatchSender.cls, at sharedKey and retryBatch.

This sends one batch. The Idempotency-Key is created once per batch and stored on each application. On a 429, a 5xx or a timeout, the same batch is retried with the same key, however long the wait, which is what FundMate confirmed: the partner ignores a repeated key for 24 hours. When a user resends after fixing something, the key is cleared, so the partner never ignores a corrected application.

UnderwritingRetryJob.cls, at execute.

The retry job resends exactly those applications after the wait: a delayed job for up to 10 minutes, a one-off scheduled job for longer. Retry-After wins, otherwise exponential backoff with jitter. After five attempts the application is marked Send Failed and goes to Integration Exceptions.

UnderwritingDecisionResource.cls, at the SignatureVerifier.verify call.

The inbound endpoint only accepts the integration user: the partner gets a token with OAuth client credentials, and any other token, even mine as admin, gets a 403. Then it checks an HMAC-SHA256 signature over the timestamp and the raw body before parsing anything. A missing, wrong or old signature gets a 401.

DecisionProcessor.cls, at the process method's steps.

Every decision gets its own result. It checks the shape, skips duplicates, matches the application and its tracking id, checks offer ids, then saves with partial success, so one bad decision never blocks the others.

That covers sending and receiving in code. Let me show decisions arriving for real.

The case

FundMate funds small businesses based on their bank cash flow. A business applies, an outside underwriting service reviews it, and a Funding Specialist works the result. The task is to build the Salesforce side of that loop.

Salesforce (what I built)Holds businesses, owners and bank statements. Builds applications, validates them, sends them, receives decisions, creates offers, routes work.
Underwriting APIOutside partner. Takes up to 50 applications per call (API key). Sends signed decisions back later.
Funding SpecialistsWork decided applications from queues. Compare offers. Can't see others' applications or change Status by hand.

FundMate's public rules used in the build: average $10,000 a month over 3 months of statements; terms 3 to 18 months; daily, weekly or monthly payments. Sample businesses are in NY, TX, FL and CA, all with commercial financing disclosure laws, so each offer shows payback, payment and an estimated APR.

Questions I asked Deepak

Is "end of day Wednesday" November 4 or 11?

November 4.

How is each request signed: header, algorithm, coverage?

Default confirmed: hex HMAC-SHA256 in X-Signature over X-Timestamp + "." + body. Older than 5 minutes rejected; 401 when missing, wrong or too old.

Does the batch endpoint take an idempotency key, so a retry after a timeout can't duplicate?

Default confirmed: reuse the same Idempotency-Key on every retry of a batch; the partner ignores a repeat for 24 hours.

Which queues exist, and what decides where an application goes?

Default confirmed (one queue per decision, over $250,000 to Senior), plus a new Integration Exceptions queue for partner rejections and send failures.

Likely interview questions

Why Batch Apex and not chained Queueables?

Each execute gets fresh limits and one callout per 50. Chained Queueables hit Developer Edition's chain depth, and one transaction allows only 120 s of callouts.

How do you stop two runs sending the same application?

FOR UPDATE locks the Ready rows, the claim re-reads them under the lock and only moves the ones still Ready to Queued with its batch key, before any callout. The second run finds nothing Ready.

What if the callout times out but the partner got it?

The retry sends the same applications with the same Idempotency-Key, so the partner ignores the repeat for 24 hours.

Why does the log survive a rollback?

Logs are published as a platform event set to Publish Immediately; a subscriber writes Integration_Log__c in its own transaction.

Why verify the signature before parsing?

The HMAC is over the raw bytes. Parsing first wastes work on forged requests and re-serialising could change the bytes.

Why are some classes without sharing?

System work must see every relevant record (claiming queue-owned apps, writing results, logging). Each of the 11 says why in its header; user-facing code runs in user mode.

What would you change for production?

Shield Platform Encryption instead of Classic, Retry-After as an HTTP date, a real partner sandbox, monitoring dashboards on Integration_Log__c, and a managed package with a namespace.

You used AI tools. Can you explain every part?

Yes. Walk through the claim, the retry key, the signature check and DecisionProcessor without notes.

Take-Home Assignment: Native Salesforce Application for Submitting, Underwriting and Routing Small-Business Funding Applications

Objective: Develop a Native Salesforce Application to create Funding Application records in Salesforce and integrate them with an external Underwriting API that reviews each application and sends a decision back. Decided applications should be queued for Funding Specialists to work.

Questions: This document intentionally leaves out some of the details you would need on a real project. Ask us, the same way you would ask a product owner before you start building. The questions you ask are part of the evaluation, so ask early and ask as many as you need. Email your questions to dev@fundmatellc.com.

Requirements:

1. Native Salesforce Application

  • Create a Salesforce Application that queries data persisting in sObjects in order to create Funding Application record(s) that get sent to an External Underwriting API. The Salesforce Application should be able to receive decisions from the External API and create Funding Offer records from them. Decided Funding Applications should be sent to various Queues for Funding Specialists to work.
  • The application should use custom objects, Apex and Flow working together. Use Flow for the parts an administrator should be able to change without a developer.

2. API Endpoint:

  • Create a Salesforce RESTful API endpoint (e.g. POST /underwriting/decisions) that accepts JSON-formatted decisions to create Funding Offer records and link them back to the Funding Application (you will have to mock the decisions sent to your API)
  • A single request may carry many decisions, and the Underwriting API may send the same decision more than once
  • Every request the Underwriting API sends is signed. Your endpoint must verify the signature and reject any request it cannot verify

3. Data Handling:

  • The Salesforce application should be able to aggregate various data from sObjects and create the request that looks similar to below:
    • Business Details (Account)
      • Legal Name
      • EIN (Tax ID)
      • State
      • Time in Business
    • Owner Details (Contact)
      • FirstName
      • LastName
      • Date of Birth
      • Social Security Number
      • Ownership Percentage
    • Financials (Bank Statements)
      • Average Monthly Deposits
      • Total NSF (returned payment) Count
      • Number of Statements
    • Funding Request (Funding Application)
      • Requested Amount
      • Use of Funds (e.g. working capital, equipment, inventory)

6. Error Handling:

  • Implement robust error handling for scenarios such as:
    • Missing required fields
    • Invalid data formats
    • External API errors (e.g. authentication, rate limiting, data validation, timeouts)
    • Duplicate decisions, and decisions for applications that cannot accept them
  • Provide meaningful error responses for Salesforce errors and consider how to handle errors related to the external API (e.g. 4xx, 429, 5xx)
  • One bad record must not cause the good records in the same request to fail
  • Logging must still record an error even when the transaction that hit it rolls back

7. Security:

  • Implement Authentication for your Salesforce RESTful endpoint, and verify the signature on every request
  • Ensure HTTP callout details and secrets are not hardcoded
  • Handle sensitive data appropriately (SSN, date of birth, EIN, bank data), ensuring compliance with relevant regulations (e.g. GLBA)

8. Data Validation:

  • Validate that each Funding Application has all the required fields and is correctly formatted before it is sent to the External API
  • Validate every decision your endpoint receives before writing it to Salesforce

9. Scale and Governor Limits:

  • The application must send 1,000 waiting Funding Applications, and accept 200 decisions in a single request, without hitting governor limits
  • The same Funding Application must never be sent twice, even when two processes run at the same time
  • Implement a retry mechanism for transient errors that respects the External API’s Retry-After header
  • Show evidence from your org that the limits held (e.g. Apex Jobs, limits usage recorded in your logs)

10. Documentation and Testing:

  • Provide clear instructions on deploying and testing the Salesforce Application
  • Include sample request payloads and describe expected responses
  • Provide unit tests with meaningful assertions (minimum 85% code coverage)

11. Demonstration Video:

  • Create a short video (5-10 minutes) highlighting:

The algorithm, end to end

A. CREATE
 1. Business (Account) with owners (Contact) and monthly Bank_Statement__c.
 2. "New Funding Application" flow inserts the application.
 3. Before insert: Status = Draft; copy the latest 3 statements into the
    snapshot fields. Formulas give average deposits and the revenue flag.

B. SUBMIT AND VALIDATE
 4. "Send to Underwriting" → SendToUnderwritingAction (reads in user mode)
    sets Status = Ready.
 5. Before update (to Ready): snapshot again, then
      errors = FundingApplicationValidator.validate(app)
      errors?  Status = Validation Failed, reasons in Status Reason. Stop.
      none?    reset Retry Count, Next Retry At, Idempotency Key.
 6. Enqueue UnderwritingDispatcher (the schedule also does this every 15 min).

C. CLAIM (never send twice)
 7. Send Enabled off → log and stop.
 8. batchKey = new UUID
    SELECT Ready (retry time passed) FOR UPDATE
    SELECT the same rows again FOR UPDATE; keep those still Ready
    UPDATE → Queued, Batch Key = batchKey        (before any callout)
    A second run waits on the lock, then finds nothing Ready.
 9. executeBatch(UnderwritingSendBatch(batchKey), 50)
    1,000 applications = 20 executes, each with its own limits, 1 callout.

D. SEND ONE BATCH
10. Load application, business, owners at 20%+, statements.
    key = the batch's shared Idempotency Key, or a new UUID.
11. Body from Payload_Field_Map__mdt.
    POST callout:Underwriting_API/v1/applications/batch
    Headers: X-Api-Key (Named Credential), Idempotency-Key; timeout 20 s.
12. 2xx: match each result by applicationReference
          Acknowledged → Status, Tracking Id, Sent At
          Rejected     → Rejected (routed to Integration Exceptions)
          missing      → back to Ready with a new key
    429 / 5xx / 408 / timeout:
          attempt = highest Retry Count + 1
          attempt ≥ Max Attempts → Send Failed, alert
          else delay = Retry-After (capped)
                       or random(0, min(base × 2^(attempt-1), cap))
               stay Queued, store Retry Count and Next Retry At
               delay ≤ 10 min → enqueueJob(RetryJob, minutes)
               delay  > 10 min → one-off scheduled job → RetryJob
               RetryJob resends the SAME applications with the SAME key
    other 4xx or bad config → Send Failed, no retry, alert
13. Database.update(..., false): one bad row doesn't block the rest.
    Log with a limits snapshot.
14. finish(): run summary; alert the owner or queue members and
    Integration Admins (bell notification and email).

E. RECEIVE  POST /services/apexrest/underwriting/decisions
15. OAuth client credentials as the integration user.
    No FundMate_Post_Underwriting_Decisions → 403.
16. Secret from the protected setting. Missing headers, timestamp off by
    more than 300 s, or HMAC-SHA256(secret, ts + "." + rawBody) not equal
    to X-Signature (constant-time) → 401.
17. Not JSON or no "decisions" array → 400. More than 500 → 413.
18. Savepoint, then DecisionProcessor:
      a. check shape and offers               → rejected with a reason
      b. decisionId seen before or repeated   → duplicate, nothing written
      c. unknown reference, wrong status, trackingId mismatch,
         second decision for one application  → rejected
      d. offerId already used                 → rejected
      e. insert decisions, then offers (allOrNone false);
         an offer fails → delete that decision and its offers
      f. mark the application's older offers Superseded
      g. application Status = decision, Decided At, Status Reason
    200 {received, created, duplicate, rejected, results[]}
    Unexpected error → rollback, log (survives), 500 "safe to retry".

F. ROUTE
19. Before-save flow: rules for this status, active, by priority;
    first where Min ≤ Requested Amount ≤ Max → OwnerId = queue.
    No match → note it in Status Reason.

G. OFFERS
20. Offer trigger sets Estimated APR. offerComparison LWC shows offers
    side by side, best value per row, superseded on request.

H. OPERATIONS
21. Errors and limits → Integration_Log_Event__e (Publish Immediately)
    → upsert Integration_Log__c on the event UUID. Text goes through

Likely questions

Why Batch Apex, not chained Queueables?

Developer Edition caps the chain depth, and one transaction gets only 120 s of callout time in total. A batch gives each 50 their own limits.

How do you stop a double send?

The claim locks with FOR UPDATE, re-reads under the lock, and moves Ready to Queued with a batch key before any callout. After a timeout the retry sends the same Idempotency-Key, and the partner ignores a repeat for 24 hours.

Why clear the key on a user resend but keep it on a retry?

A resend may carry corrected data, so it's a new request. A retry is the same request.

Why log through a platform event?

A normal insert rolls back with the transaction. A Publish Immediately event is delivered anyway.

Why OAuth and HMAC both?

OAuth proves who is calling. HMAC proves the body wasn't changed and isn't a replay.

Why 11 classes without sharing?

System processes must see private, queue-owned records. Each one has a reason in the README. The user entry points, the Send action and the offer controller, run in user mode.

Why is date of birth a text field?

Classic Encryption only works on text. A regex validation rule enforces the format.

What changed after your questions email?

Statements moved from the application to the Account, with a snapshot on the application, because they exist before any application. Needs More Info can take a second decision that supersedes older offers. Rejections and send failures got their own Integration Exceptions queue, from the product owner's answer.

The one-paragraph answer

Apex does the work that needs code: the integration, scale, security and logging. A send run claims Ready applications under a lock, then a batch sends them 50 per callout through a Named Credential and writes the result back on each record. Why is routing not in Apex?

The brief says admin-changeable logic belongs in Flow. Route_Decided_Funding_Application reads Routing_Rule__mdt, so queues and thresholds change in Setup.

Why split UnderwritingSendBatch and UnderwritingBatchSender?

The batch handles scope and run totals; the sender handles one batch. The retry job reuses the sender, so first sends and retries follow the same rules.

You can't make a callout after DML in the same transaction, and the lock has to be released before the send so the second run can see the rows are Queued.
